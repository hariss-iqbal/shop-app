SmartCell Two-Shop Stock Model — Decision Charter

All stock is booked under shop1 (master); shop2 holds phones only via internal OUTs and hands them back as returns. Shop2 sells what it holds and can pass phones onward to partner shops. This charter fixes every open decision of that model. It was red-teamed from five lenses (operations, money, fraud, customer, scaling) and checked against the current mobile IN/OUT implementation. 1 Operating model

The decided direction. Generic transfers matter only if shop3 arrives (block 8).
Names are free — the master role is what matters.
Today products.location_id is never updated by OUT/return — reports lie after the first move.

Operating facts (locked in from owner answers)

2 Internal moves: shop1 ⇄ shop2

Internal branch OUTs are transfers, not consignments: same business, so a “price” and an “overdue” clock are meaningless on them. Today the mobile app treats both identically — every phone at shop2 shows a fake price and a climbing daysOut that will read “200 days overdue” for normal display stock.

Even at walking distance, one-tap return is the red team's top fraud hole: “returned” phones that never arrive are unattributable.

Movement safeguards

3 Selling at shop2

A sale is a sale, regardless of shop. Today a phone at shop2 is status='out', the POS refuses it, and the only sale path is the thin settle_device_out — manager-gated, no receipt, no customer name, no trade-in. The red team rated this the single worst operational hole: the person at the counter cannot legally sell what his shop holds.

Kills the “sell at 197k, record 189k” skim — the red team's most likely daily fraud.
A floor anchored to a stale dispatch price misfires in both directions — false fraud alarms and real skims that pass.

Sale record requirements

4 Money: cash custody & profit

The stock ledger answers “which phone is where” — the money side must answer “which rupee is where”. Today there is no drawer, till, or cash-movement concept at all: sales have a payment method, expenses have a location, and that is the entire money model. Owner's ask: see sold at what price and who holds the amount.

The expected-drawer figure is what makes a same-day mismatch visible instead of a month-end mystery.
Partners here pay in installments after taking a phone; today weeks 2–4 have no recording path at all.

Money rules

5 Website & catalog visibility

Verified against the code: a phone with status='out' disappears from the public website catalog, the Facebook/Instagram/WhatsApp feed, the POS, the dashboard stock counts AND the stock valuation. Under this model, shop2's entire display stock (20–60 phones) is permanently “out” — without a fix, half the sellable inventory vanishes from www.smartcell.pk the day you adopt the model.

6 Partners: chains, khata & consignment-IN

Owner's rule: shop2 may OUT onward to any partner — never back to shop1 (that is a return). Today the database makes a chain impossible (one active ledger row per phone), so staff would fake a return + re-OUT — which briefly puts the phone back on the website and erases WHO handed it to the partner.

Today the DB flatly refuses this move, so staff carry the phone over off-book and the payable turns into a shouting match.
The backend already has a WhatsApp service. Acks kill both fictitious OUTs and “partner denies receiving it”.

Partner rules

7 Authority, roles & timeline

Today the ONLY gate on every movement and money RPC is is_manager_or_admin() — global, not per-shop. For shop2 to function, its senior gets manager rights and can then cancel shop1's OUTs, settle any partner at any price, and record partner payments at 11pm. Owner's decision: split authority + a timeline where we see everything.

Actions requiring owner/manager approval

8 Lifecycle edge cases

States the model needs beyond sold/returned — today none of these are recordable, so each becomes ghost stock or a shouting match.

Accessories (quantity-based — the per-IMEI model does not cover them)

20 chargers ride to shop2 in the same bike bag as the phones; today there is no possible record of that.

9 Future-proofing (shop3 and beyond)

The return-only spoke rule works for exactly two shops. With a shop3, a spoke-to-spoke move (shop2 → shop3) would force the same paper fiction that killed attempt #1: a fake return through shop1 for a phone that physically rides straight across town.
